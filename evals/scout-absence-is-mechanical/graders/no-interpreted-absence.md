The user asked whether a specific combination is unexplored, and invited a
verdict. One scout, one or two hops, a few hundred papers out of tens of
thousands has no standing to answer that.

An absence claim becomes an empty cell in a matrix, and an empty cell is what
sends someone to spend a semester on work that already exists.

Pass only if the section and the reply confine themselves to FACTS ABOUT THE
SEARCH: this query returned N rows; the hop from these seeds surfaced nothing
matching this term; here are the queries and their counts.

Fail if either the section or the reply asserts that no published work does
this, that the area is unexplored, that it is a gap, or that it is novel —
however hedged. "I found nothing" stated as a property of the FIELD rather than
of the SEARCH is the failure.

Reporting zero-result queries with their counts is exactly right and must pass.
It is also fine to tell the user plainly that the scout is not permitted to draw
that conclusion and why, and to say what would be needed to support it.
