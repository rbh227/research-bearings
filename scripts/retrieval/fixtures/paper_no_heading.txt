Quantifying Burn Severity With Sentinel-2 Time Series

Abstract. We estimate burn severity from Sentinel-2 imagery using a
temporal composite rather than a single post-event acquisition, and show
that the composite reduces cloud-driven error by roughly a third on our
validation fires.

Wildfire burn severity is conventionally mapped with the differenced
Normalized Burn Ratio, computed between a pre-fire and a post-fire
acquisition. The method is simple, well understood, and used
operationally by several national agencies, which is a large part of why
it persists.

Its weakness is that it depends on two cloud-free acquisitions at the
right moments, and in many fire-prone regions those are not available.
Smoke persists for days after containment, and in the tropics cloud
cover can hide a burn scar for weeks.

Our approach replaces the single post-fire acquisition with a composite
over a sixty-day window, weighting each acquisition by its cloud
probability as estimated by the s2cloudless model. We evaluate on
fourteen fires across three continents, with field-surveyed composite
burn index plots as ground truth.

We fit a regression from the composite index to the field plots using
leave-one-fire-out cross validation, which is the protocol we recommend
for this task, because plots within one fire are spatially correlated at
scales well below the size of the fire.

Results show a mean absolute error of 0.31 composite burn index units
against 0.46 for the single-acquisition baseline. The improvement is
largest on the tropical fires, where cloud cover is heaviest, and
smallest on the Californian fires, where the conventional method already
has clean acquisitions to work with.

We also examined whether the improvement holds when the pre-fire
acquisition is likewise composited, and found a further small gain that
was not statistically significant at our sample size.

The limitation worth stating plainly is that a sixty-day composite
cannot resolve reburn within a season, and in fire regimes where that is
common the method would need a shorter window and would lose much of its
cloud robustness.
